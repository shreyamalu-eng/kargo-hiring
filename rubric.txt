KARGO HIRING RUBRIC  -  Product Manager (PM) and Senior Product Manager (SPM)
==========================================================================
Derived from the 8 past-hire profiles in hires/ and the outcome ratings in the case brief.
The job descriptions were used only to understand the roles - no criterion is taken from them.

STEP 1 - PATTERNS: what the "Exceeds Expectations" hires share that the others lack
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Exceeds:   Rohan Desai, Sunita Krishnamurthy, Aditya Shetty, Meghna Tiwari, Lavanya Iyer
Meets:     Vikram Nair (PM), Rahul Bose (Growth)
Below:     Preetham Rao (Backend)

Pattern 1 - They did the operational work themselves before building for it.
  All five Exceeds hires spent real time on an operations floor handling shipments,
  documentation, carriers or clients with their own hands:
  Rohan (Operations Executive at a CHA firm, JNPT - 180+ shipments/month),
  Sunita (documentation for 200+ shipments/month at Trident Freight),
  Aditya (port services at JNPT - berth windows, DO releases, detention),
  Meghna (freight forwarder documentation + LC shipments at Coastline),
  Lavanya (carrier allocation and exception management at Mahindra Logistics).
  None of the Meets/Below hires did: Vikram (HR-tech SaaS only), Rahul (fintech marketing),
  Preetham (integrated 3PL APIs from an e-commerce backend - built FOR logistics, never IN it).

Pattern 2 - They built things nobody asked for, and other people adopted them.
  Rohan: Excel shipment tracker adopted by the 12-person ops team in two weeks; BoL
  verification prototype built over a weekend, 30 colleagues using it within a month.
  Sunita: redesigned the documentation workflow over a weekend when the FMS vendor changed
  format - retained permanently. Lavanya: visibility dashboard adopted by 2 other regional
  teams. Meghna: 30-60-90 onboarding framework now used by the whole CS team. Aditya: first
  case-study programme, now the team's primary collateral.
  Weaker or absent: Vikram (PRD template inside his own PM remit), Preetham (monitoring
  dashboard, no adoption signal), Rahul (assigned programmes).

Pattern 3 - They own misses openly and turn them into something others use.
  Lavanya killed 2 of her own features on usage data and wrote the outage post-mortem.
  Aditya wrote a post-mortem on a 4-month lost deal that became standard practice.
  Meghna fixed a customs hold overnight and wrote the bug report for a product gap.
  Rohan led a vendor migration under time pressure with no data loss.
  Vikram's and Rahul's CVs contain only wins and rising metrics; Preetham's incidents are
  an assigned on-call rotation, not an owned miss.

Supporting signals (used as lighter-weight criteria):
  - Direct line to the user, no layer in between (Rohan "without a product layer",
    Aditya "no account manager layer", Meghna primary contact, Lavanya's discovery pivot)
    vs Vikram presenting roadmaps up to CTO/VP Sales.
  - Makes the call (Lavanya's engineering lead: "made calls we trusted immediately. She
    doesn't hedge.") vs Vikram "socialised", "managed competing stakeholder priorities".

SCORING SCALE (every criterion)
-------------------------------
  5 = strong, specific evidence exactly as described
  3-4 = partial evidence (some of it, or less specific)
  1-2 = weak or indirect evidence
  0 = no evidence in the CV
Weighted total (0-100) = sum over criteria of (weight x score / 5).

==========================================================================
ROLE: PM
==========================================================================

Criterion name: Hands-on Operations Time
Key: hands_on_ops
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done operational work in freight, logistics, ports, supply chain or a similarly operations-heavy floor - handled shipments, documentation, carriers, customs or exceptions themselves - for at least a year, and the CV names the specifics (Bills of Lading, CHAs, carrier allocation, berth windows, exception queues). Partial (2-3): built software for operations users, or embedded/visited with them, but never did the work. Weak (0-1): operations knowledge only from software, consulting, or interviews.
Weight: 30%
Source: Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya (all Exceeds) vs Vikram, Rahul, Preetham.

Criterion name: Unasked Builds That Got Adopted
Key: unasked_builds
What a strong candidate looks like: Noticed a gap nobody assigned them, built a fix on their own initiative (a tracker, prototype, SOP, framework, template or process), and other people adopted it - with the adoption named (e.g. "adopted by the 12-person team within two weeks", "now the team standard"). Partial (2-3): improvements inside their assigned scope, or self-started builds with no adoption evidence. Weak (0-1): only assigned deliverables.
Weight: 25%
Source: Rohan's Excel tracker and weekend BoL prototype; Sunita's weekend workflow redesign; Lavanya's dashboard; Meghna's onboarding framework; Aditya's case-study programme.

Criterion name: Owns the Miss
Key: owns_the_miss
What a strong candidate looks like: Describes a specific thing that went wrong - a killed feature, a lost deal, an outage, a crisis - and shows they owned it personally and turned it into a fix or a documented lesson others now use (post-mortem shared, feature killed with the data behind it, practice adopted). Partial (2-3): handled a crisis but no lesson captured, or generic "managed incidents". Weak (0-1): the CV contains only wins and rising metrics.
Weight: 20%
Source: Lavanya's killed features and outage post-mortem; Aditya's lost-deal post-mortem; Meghna's overnight customs fix and bug report. Absent in Vikram and Rahul.

Criterion name: Direct Line to the User
Key: direct_line
What a strong candidate looks like: Worked face to face with the people who use the work, with no intermediary layer, and changed what got built because of it - e.g. "no product layer", embedded with ops teams, primary client contact, and a concrete change that came from it. Partial (2-3): user research through structured interviews, surveys or analytics. Weak (0-1): works through stakeholders and presents roadmaps upward; no direct user contact.
Weight: 15%
Source: Rohan ("without a product layer"), Aditya ("no account manager layer"), Meghna (primary contact, 12 accounts), Lavanya (discovery session that caused a pivot) vs Vikram.

Criterion name: Makes the Call
Key: makes_the_call
What a strong candidate looks like: Makes decisions and acts on them quickly without waiting for approval or consensus - killed their own work, redirected capacity, chose a path under pressure - or has a third-party quote about decisiveness. Partial (2-3): executed decisions that others made. Weak (0-1): language centred on "aligned", "socialised", "supported", "managed stakeholder priorities".
Weight: 10%
Source: Lavanya ("She doesn't hedge"), Sunita (independent weekend redesign), Rohan (called the vendor migration) vs Vikram ("socialised").

==========================================================================
ROLE: SPM
==========================================================================
Same criteria, higher bar - especially on how independently the person has operated.

Criterion name: Hands-on Operations Time
Key: hands_on_ops
What a strong candidate looks like: Two or more years doing operational work themselves (freight, ports, customs, carriers, supply chain) AND at least one example where that ground knowledge shaped a technical, data or integration decision later (e.g. "designed the integration because I knew what the data actually contained"). Partial (2-3): hands-on operations time with no link to later product decisions, or under a year of it. Weak (0-1): no hands-on operations time.
Weight: 25%
Source: Rohan (CHA operations to engineering lead on freight tracking), Lavanya, Sunita.

Criterion name: Unasked Builds That Got Adopted
Key: unasked_builds
What a strong candidate looks like: Self-initiated builds whose adoption spread beyond their own team - to other teams, other shifts, other branches or the whole company - or became a core product feature. Partial (2-3): self-started builds adopted only within their own team. Weak (0-1): only assigned deliverables.
Weight: 15%
Source: Rohan (weekend prototype became a core platform feature); Lavanya (dashboard adopted by 2 other regional teams).

Criterion name: Owns the Miss
Key: owns_the_miss
What a strong candidate looks like: Owned failure at the level of a product area or function - killed features including their own ideas, reversed a direction on evidence, ran the post-mortem and changed how the team works as a result. Partial (2-3): owned individual incidents or kills without a lasting change to practice. Weak (0-1): only wins and rising metrics.
Weight: 20%
Source: Lavanya, Aditya.

Criterion name: Direct Line to the User
Key: direct_line
What a strong candidate looks like: Even at a senior level still personally in the room with customers or operators (onboardings, discovery, field visits) with a named decision that came from it. Partial (2-3): contact through their team, research reports or account managers. Weak (0-1): no direct contact at senior level.
Weight: 10%
Source: Rohan, Aditya, Lavanya.

Criterion name: Makes the Call
Key: makes_the_call
What a strong candidate looks like: Has owned a product area or function with no senior layer above them for 2+ years, AND made at least one high-consequence, hard-to-reverse call themselves (architecture, build vs configure vs not touch, killing a product line) and lived with the result. Partial (2-3): made calls inside a structure where a senior PM or committee had final say. Weak (0-1): mainly executed or "aligned" on others' decisions.
Weight: 30%
Source: Lavanya ("made calls we trusted immediately"), Rohan (most senior engineer, led migration), Sunita (independent consultant owning client outcomes).
